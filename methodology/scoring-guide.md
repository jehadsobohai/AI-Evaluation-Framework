## Purpose

This guide provides practical guidance for designing, applying, and interpreting scoring methodologies within the AI Evaluation Framework.

It complements the **Evaluation Project Standard (EPS)** by explaining how evaluation scores should be assigned, justified, interpreted, and reported while maintaining transparency, consistency, and reproducibility.

---

## Scope

This guide applies to AI evaluation projects that use qualitative assessment, quantitative scoring, comparative ranking, benchmark-based evaluation, automatic metrics, or hybrid evaluation methodologies.

It is intended to support evaluations across multiple AI systems, domains, tasks, and benchmarks.

---

## Scoring Principles

Scoring methodologies should adhere to the following principles:

- Consistency
- Transparency
- Reproducibility
- Evidence-Based Assessment
- Objectivity
- Fairness
- Traceability

Scores should always be supported by documented evidence and interpreted within the context of the evaluation objectives.

---

## Qualitative Scoring

Qualitative scoring relies on expert judgment rather than numerical metrics.

Examples include:

- Excellent
- Good
- Acceptable
- Needs Improvement
- Poor

Qualitative assessments should include:

- Clear evaluation criteria
- Supporting observations
- Evidence-based justification
- Documented reasoning

---

## Quantitative Scoring

Quantitative scoring assigns numerical values to evaluation criteria.

Examples include:

- Binary (Pass / Fail)
- 1–3 Scale
- 1–5 Scale
- 1–10 Scale
- Percentage Scores
- Benchmark Metrics (e.g., Accuracy, Precision, Recall, COMET)

Projects should clearly document:

- The scoring scale
- Score definitions
- Interpretation guidelines
- Aggregation methods (if applicable)

---

## Hybrid Scoring

Many AI evaluations combine qualitative and quantitative assessment.

Examples include:

- Human Evaluation + COMET
- Human Evaluation + BLEU
- Benchmark Scores + Expert Review
- Automatic Metrics + Error Analysis

Hybrid methodologies provide both measurable performance indicators and expert interpretation.

---

## Evidence and Justification

Every assigned score should be supported by documented evidence.

Evidence may include:

- Model responses
- Benchmark results
- Evaluation datasets
- Automatic metrics
- Human observations
- Error analysis
- Comparative findings

Evaluation reports should clearly distinguish between:

- Objective observations
- Quantitative measurements
- Expert interpretations
- Recommendations

---

## Best Practices

When designing and applying scoring methodologies:

- Define scoring criteria before evaluation begins.
- Apply scoring consistently across all evaluated systems.
- Document all scoring scales and interpretation guidelines.
- Support conclusions with observable evidence.
- Avoid subjective judgments without justification.
- Report assumptions and limitations.
- Preserve reproducibility through transparent documentation.
